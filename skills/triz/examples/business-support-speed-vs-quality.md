# Example: business - faster support without sloppy answers

- Domain: business
- Mode: guided

## Problem as the user stated it

> Our support team has to answer tickets faster, but when we push for speed
> the answers get sloppy and customers write back.

## Conversation

**Stage 1, intake:** First reply takes about 9 hours; the target is under 1.
A 1-hour target raised the reopen rate from 8% to 19%. No new hires this
year; the team uses Zendesk; senior agents are the best asset. IFR: "every
customer gets a correct answer at once, with no extra staff." Resources:
Zendesk macros and triggers, last quarter's ticket history, seniors'
knowledge. The user confirms.

**Stage 2, contradiction:** Cause chain: reopens rose, because agents reply
before they understand the ticket, because every ticket gets the same 1-hour
target. TC: improving reply speed worsens answer reliability. PC: the reply
must be instant (fast) and considered (correct). Confirmed.

**Stage 3, directions:** Speed could be time customers wait (25 Loss of time)
or tickets per hour (39 Productivity); quality is 27 Reliability. Merging
25x27 and 39x27 gives #10 Preliminary action twice, then #30, #4, #1, #35,
#38. The agent leads with #10 and #1 Segmentation, and drops #30 Flexible
shells and thin films as too far from the problem.

**Stage 4, actions:**
1. Seniors write macros for the 20 most common ticket types, about 60% of
   volume (#10). IFR fit high, effort low.
2. A Zendesk trigger routes tickets that match a macro to an "instant" queue
   and the rest to "investigate", with an honest 4-hour promise (#1). IFR fit
   high, effort low.
3. An intake form asks for the order number and a screenshot first (#10).
   IFR fit medium, effort low.

**Stage 5, validation:** "Who would object to option 1, and why?" Seniors may
hate writing canned replies. The agent asks whether owning them would change
that; the user says seniors would like owning the quality numbers, so each
senior owns five macros and reviews their reopen rate weekly. "What does this
make harder later?" Macros go stale; the weekly review covers it. Approved.

## Short plan

**Short plan: support speed without losing quality**
**Contradiction:** improving reply speed worsens answer reliability (instant and considered)
**Chosen solution:** senior-owned macros plus split queues (principles #10 Preliminary action, #1 Segmentation)
**Next steps:**
1. Pull the top 20 ticket types from last quarter (team lead, Friday)
2. Each senior writes and owns five macros (seniors, in 2 weeks)
3. Add the instant/investigate trigger in Zendesk (admin, in 2 weeks)
**Resources:** Zendesk macros and triggers, last quarter's ticket history, seniors' time (about 2 hours each); new: nothing
**Watch for:** reopen rate on macro tickets above 8% for a week, then pull that macro for review
**Done when:** median first reply under 1 hour with reopen rate at or below 8%, within 6 weeks

## What made this work

The viewpoints question turned the main objection into ownership, which is
also what keeps the macros correct over time.
